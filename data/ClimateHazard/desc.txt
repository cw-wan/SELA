NOTE: every sample in this dataset carries the same 7-channel array regardless
of class — [VIS_km, TD_SPREAD, TEMP_C, DEWP_C, WND_KT, WIND_CHILL, SLP_hPa] —
so the class can never be inferred from which channels are present. Each class
entry below only narrates the subset of those 7 channels that is physically
diagnostic for that hazard; the remaining channels are still present in the
data but simply uninformative (e.g. near-zero) for that class.

NOTE: WIND_CHILL doubles as the heat-index channel. Wind chill and heat index
are both "feels-like temperature" derived from the same TEMP_C/DEWP_C/WND_KT
inputs, and their domains of validity never overlap (wind chill only defined
when TEMP_C ≤ 10 °C, heat index only defined when TEMP_C ≥ 26.7 °C) — so one
shared column unambiguously carries the cold-side value in cold events, the
hot-side value in hot events, and 0.0 in the mild band between where neither
applies.

### 1. Dense Fog (dense_fog)
Channels: [VIS_km, TD_SPREAD, TEMP_C, DEWP_C, WND_KT]

Dense fog occurs when suspended water droplets reduce surface visibility to 
≤ 0.40 km. It forms when air temperature and dewpoint 
converge to near-identical values.

NOTE: All channels exhibit measurement noise and physical oscillations. Evaluate 
the dominant sustained pattern, not individual outlier readings. Transient 
deviations of 1–2 hours should not alter event boundaries.

>> Event criteria (definitional):

   VIS_km: Drops abruptly from ambient (≥ 5 km) to ≤ 0.40 km, then sustains 
   a near-zero floor (0.05–0.20 km). Brief spikes above threshold (≤ 1.0 km, 
   1–2 readings) are noise — do not treat as event termination.

   TD_SPREAD: Collapses from baseline (3–8 °C) to near zero (0–1 °C) and 
   remains pinched. Minor oscillations (±0.5 °C) are expected within this 
   saturated regime.

   TEMP_C / DEWP_C: Converge to within ≤ 1 °C and track in near-parallel 
   throughout. Brief crossings or ±0.5 °C fluctuations are normal sensor 
   rounding, not event boundaries.

### 2. Extreme Cold / Wind Chill (extreme_cold_wind_chill)
Channels: [TEMP_C, WIND_CHILL, SLP_hPa]

Extreme Cold/Wind Chill is defined as a period of extremely low temperatures or wind
chill values reaching warning criteria: TEMP_C ≤ −18 °C OR WIND_CHILL ≤ −28 °C (NWS
Instruction 10-1605). Either condition alone is sufficient — cold is dangerous with or
without elevated wind. WIND_CHILL is a derived quantity computed from TEMP_C and wind
speed; it is only defined when TEMP_C ≤ 10 °C and wind speed ≥ ~4.8 km/h, otherwise
it is None (filled as 0.0 in the timeseries).

NOTE: All channels exhibit measurement noise and diurnal oscillations. Evaluate the
dominant sustained pattern. Transient recoveries of 1–2 hours should not alter event
boundaries.

>> Event criteria (definitional):

   TEMP_C: Drops sharply from pre-event baseline and sustains extreme lows (≤ −18 °C).
   Shape: rapid descent marking Arctic air-mass arrival, then sustained cold plateau.
   Diurnal oscillations (±2 °C) are noise — assess the dominant sustained level, not
   individual outlier readings.

   WIND_CHILL: Diverges below TEMP_C when wind is elevated, sustaining ≤ −28 °C
   throughout. When wind is calm, WIND_CHILL ≈ TEMP_C at extreme lows. Minor
   oscillations tracking wind fluctuations are expected and do not indicate event
   termination. TEMP_C or WIND_CHILL meeting threshold is sufficient (OR logic);
   both need not be present simultaneously.

   SLP_hPa: Rises sharply as Arctic air arrives, sustaining elevated values (> 1020 hPa,
   often 1030–1050+ hPa) throughout the event, then gradually declines as the system
   exits. Shape: sharp rise → sustained high plateau → gradual post-event decline.
   Short-term oscillations (±2 hPa) are normal.

### 3. Frost/Freeze (frost_freeze)
Channels: [TEMP_C, DEWP_C, TD_SPREAD, WND_KT]

Frost/Freeze covers overnight radiational-cooling events: a Freeze Warning
(temperature drops to or below freezing) or the milder Frost Advisory (near-
freezing under clear skies and calm wind), typically during growing-season
shoulder months (spring/fall) rather than the deep winter cold air masses of
Extreme Cold/Wind Chill.

>> Event criteria (definitional):

   TEMP_C: Descends overnight to ≤ 2 °C. Shape is diurnal-radiational — steady
   descent through the night, minimum near sunrise, recovery afterward — rather
   than the abrupt arctic-airmass-arrival shape of Extreme Cold.

   DEWP_C / TD_SPREAD: Efficient radiational cooling requires dry air, so
   TD_SPREAD should stay wide (dry) rather than collapsing toward zero the way
   Dense Fog's does; a spread that narrows to near-zero as TEMP_C nears DEWP_C
   indicates fog/frost-point conditions rather than dry radiational cooling.

   WND_KT: Stays low/calm throughout — wind mixing suppresses radiational
   cooling, so a genuine Frost/Freeze night shows persistently light wind,
   distinguishing it from wind-driven cold-air-advection events.
